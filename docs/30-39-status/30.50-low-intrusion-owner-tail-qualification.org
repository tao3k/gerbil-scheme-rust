* Observation scope and matched control

=--probe-observation= retains nine entry marks and three native snapshots per
short request. Snapshots bracket construction and the pending interval before
projection. They report owner thread CPU, native GC wall/count and roots; GC
user/system counters are process scoped and must not be added across owners.
There are no per-bulk diagnostic reads, whole-owner phase histories, Mach state
polls, calibration workloads or native select/sleep recording in this mode.
The native anchor-only control disables both logical and native wait tracing;
readback rejects any retained wait event. Snapshot counts reflect actual
availability and are asserted, rather than inferred from the requested mode.

=--observer-ab= uses the actual pooled native API in both arms: four live VMs,
two Tokio workers, batch eight, cooperative budget one, NativePort, feed one,
coalesced notification, progress-aware routing, four slots/work 32 per owner
and three active bulk callbacks per owner. Both retain the native short layout.
Only short-request observation differs. Both arms use matching out-of-timing
Scheme clock fences, avoiding unequal control allocations before each sample.
The timed Off arm has no probe counters or entry marks. This is distinct from
an ordinary unobserved run, which does not execute those fences at all.

Each sample retains 8192 native orders with 48 lines, 64 four-line serial short
probes and their nominal 500 us schedule. Actual latency starts before admission;
scheduled latency also contains delayed arrivals. The mixed workload retains
actual Scheme waits. =--blocks=50= repeats the normal alternating AB/BA pair ten
times more often than default; the same four VMs survive the whole capture.
There are 100 samples and 6400 short requests per arm/load, 25600 short requests
per long capture. The native progress gate remains five seconds.

* Construction, GC and waiting are different costs

The largest A request (8.392 ms) spent 8.199 ms after admission before owner
entry. Its wake write completed in microseconds; construction was 22.250 us,
pending 144.416 us and projection 2.709 us. GC counters did not advance between
its three snapshots. Several owners appear in the same outlier cluster. A
successful/coalesced wake is not evidence that an owner was idle or ran promptly.

There is also genuine GC-associated construction in A: one construction took
796.166 us wall, 795.542 us owner CPU, 784.874 us GC wall and one collection.
27 of 12800 observed short constructions crossed a collection. This cost is
material but does not explain the largest pre-entry cluster. GC before the
first snapshot remains outside per-probe counter attribution.

B's largest request took 39.335 ms: 8.356 ms before owner entry and 30.887 ms
in the construction span. In Probe this span includes two diagnostic reads;
their individual costs were not yet recorded in these frozen captures. It
cannot be narrowed to time inside the constructor. The span consumed only
49.917 us of owner
CPU and had zero GC count/wall increment. It therefore cannot be called 30 ms
of allocation/GC computation. The second-largest observed request spent
19.888 ms before entry, with only 16.583 us construction. Counter evidence
separates non-CPU wall delay from execution, but cannot distinguish Runnable
wait, a blocking lock, or another off-core cause for these exact requests.

The fixture reports GC count and heap/live capacity through existing native
interfaces, not an exact allocated-byte counter for each constructor. Heap
capacity must not be relabeled allocation volume. No unverified allocator
formula or Gambit internal layout dependency was added.

* Separating the observer's own wall cost

The final fixture adds =probe_native_snapshot_ns=: three explicit wall durations
around the existing diagnostic calls. No new native counter read is introduced.
Off emits null entries and makes no timing calls for these snapshots. The two
construction diagnostic reads remain within the original construction span;
the third is within projection. Their durations must not be called constructor
or projector work. Snapshot CPU/GC attribution still has its original boundary.

A final same-VM 50-block Off/Probe capture (36.23 s) validated 12800 observed
triples, matching absent Off entries and both construction reads bounded by the
construction wall span. Its paired results are below; this is a distinct source
identity from A/B above.

* Kernel evidence and its remaining limit

Two Probe-only full-payload =--trace-once= captures completed recorder/export
with target exit zero, four owner TIDs, 16 timed anchors and coverage of all
256 ingress intervals each. Native monotonic resolution was 1 us; intersected
offset uncertainty was +/-1.125 us, before adding each request's clock anchor
uncertainty and another native tick. Missing/overlapping intervals remain
rejected. Both used the qualified five-second rolling window and reduced
Time Profiler plus Thread State Trace recipe, without the full in-process
phase observer.

The first's largest ingress was 799 us: 759.459 us Running, 10.583 us
Interrupted and 28.958 us Preempted. The second's largest ingress was 344.084 us,
all Running at the trace's resolution. Neither captured the long-run 20-39 ms
requests. The two kernel captures are local scheduling evidence for their own
requests, not a basis for transferring state labels onto another capture.
Whole-run blocked totals include startup, idle time and cleanup and are not
request service delays. Kernel-traced throughput is not performance acceptance.
Natural getrusage caller resolution reached =___process_times= but the recorded
stack did not retain its native GC callers. Those syscalls are therefore not
admitted as paired GC boundaries; their presence alone does not prove GC.

* Consequence for routing and supply

The admission-to-dequeue stage is where the largest A tails occurred. Some B
construction spikes instead consist almost entirely of non-CPU delay. Routing
cannot safely treat either a completed wake or a historical phase label as a
current execution-duration prediction. Moving an already accepted rooted job
between owners would violate the native ownership boundary.

The prior phase-routing candidate remains withdrawn. This round does not
introduce another speculative router, GC policy or completion-retirement
budget: no measurement here attributes the largest tail to result projection
or proves that changing those policies fixes the off-core interval. Production
routing/supply optimization remains open until an actual long-tail request has
covered scheduler/blocking evidence or another directly measured protocol cause.
All negative runs are retained; no default is promoted on a quiet subcapture.

A separate existing fixed-owner versus pooled-supply Probe capture retained its
negative CPU throughput result (415781 -> 408843 orders/s, -1.67%) and modest
mixed result (66471 -> 66850, +0.57%). Mixed p99 changed 0.569 -> 0.485 ms; CPU
p99 0.429 -> 0.433 ms. This is a directed existing-policy comparison, not a new
algorithm speedup and not transferable to the degraded capture B.
