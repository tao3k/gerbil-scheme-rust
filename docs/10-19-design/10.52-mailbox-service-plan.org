1. A post-GC callback timestamp is an observation after finalization. Even

* Collector pauses and the unchanged 128-debt budget

Total CPU cost and peak debt impose different requirements. A brief pause can
violate the debt limit even when collector CPU is a small part of the whole
run. More ready green threads on the same stopped processor cannot consume
messages during that interval. Faster recovery afterwards cannot erase the
peak that already occurred. This is a necessary-budget question, not a claim
that GC caused the original 6.497 ms difference or every observed peak.

The retained published reader source matches the d56f94c Git blob.
Later disassembly of the pinned original image shows that diagnostics
reads counters around a root-count callback into Scheme, which can collect.
The earlier counter-only assumption is withdrawn. Before-snapshot collector
time can enter the wall delta from outside the timed call. Under monotone
counters and complete coverage the count can upper-bound the relevant
collection count even when it includes after-snapshot events. Establish
those premises separately. Collector wall time and the arrival clock also
need a bound on their historical accumulated mismatch. The model rejects
a certified debt floor without verified coverage and bounds.

Conditional arithmetic: if G is collector wall time, W whole-call duration,
O the complete offering span and E a PROVEN aggregate overstatement allowance,
collector overlap inside offering is at least max(0,G-E-(W-O)). With K
nonoverlapping collector pauses, at least one overlap is ceil(that/K). Convert
that interval into a conservative due-arrival count, excluding both endpoints
and allowing one boundary retirement. This does not reconstruct a GC timeline.

| Historical record | GC wall / count | Whole call outside offering | Inside-offer lower, assuming E=0 | Strict E ceiling forcing >128, with one boundary return |
|-------------------+-----------------+-----------------------------+---------------------------------+-------------------------------------------------------|
| Published 4.513 ms | 17.893791 ms / 39 | 9.153667 ms | 8.740124 ms | E < 3.670124 ms |
| Raw 3.927 ms | 16.150475 ms / 40 | 10.853000 ms | 5.297474 ms | E < 0.097474 ms |

These are conditional requirements, not certified GC-attributed failures:
no historical E bound has been established. At a hypothetical verified E of
1 ms, the first record would force at least 197 due-unconsumed during one
collector overlap even allowing the boundary return. The second record would
not force a breach at that E. This sensitivity must remain visible; assume
neither perfect clock alignment nor causality from the counts alone.

The published 4.513 ms metrics are the Git-retained structured record; its raw
stdout hash is retained but those bytes were not revalidated in this audit.
The 3.927 ms and other four-round stdout bytes were rehashed against the
existing inventory. All rows are retained in
=.ci/mailbox-send-target-20261107/gc-offer-budget-20261108/receipt.json=.

This adds a separate design obligation for the native service cycle: bound

** Source accounting and sampling boundary

At each driver sample, due-unconsumed = (due - published) + (published -
verified-consumed). The second term is bounded by the three native frames.
The maxima of the two reported series can occur at different iterations.
At a sampled total peak of 724966, at least 724963 requests are still
unpublished. The two reported maxima differ by two, which does not identify
native occupancy at either peak. The model retains a counterexample in
which that difference is one and the total peak holds two native frames.
This accounting localizes outstanding ownership. It does not establish
whether late publication followed a wait for an occupied frame, slow
execution or delayed owner scheduling.

For op62, the source obtains planned timestamps from the owned plan and
uses CLOCK_UPTIME_RAW for elapsed and observed consumption timestamps.
The worker verifies the result before marking the frame ready. The source
samples latency after checking completion identity and failure and before
updating the histogram, marking verified progress, and releasing the frame.
The reported quantiles are nearest-rank bucket upper edges at 1 us
resolution. The histogram allocation and worker startup precede the inner
clock. Percentile scanning follows the inner stop and falls inside the
whole native call. Peak debt is sampled at the top of the drive loop,
and samples_over_original_window counts loop observations. It does not
measure a per-message violation count or duration. A quantile cannot be
independently reconstructed from these peak and elapsed summaries.

For a successful complete op62 call, under the existing due-count formula,
the continuous outstanding peak lies between the sampled peak and the
sampled peak plus one (or one if no sample has been observed). Between two top-of-drive samples, due is monotone and at most one
frame is verified-consumed, so the next sample can be at most one below
any intermediate peak. In the final iteration, at most one request
remains, because the only successful exit follows the last retirement.
The bound uses the existing clocks and counters with no hot-path change.
For a strict improvement, compare the candidate's sampled peak plus one
with the control's sampled peak. A 126 versus 127 sampled pair can still
tie at a true peak of 127. The replay gate uses this conservative
comparison against both the published reference and matched control.
The original 128 gate remains a separate check.
At the integral benchmark cadences, a sampled peak of 127 certifies
the 128 limit for this successful path. A sampled 128 cannot distinguish 128 from 129. An
observed 129 already fails. A terminal exit may lack the next sample and
does not inherit this bound. Its explicit failure cut remains separate.

This refines the interpretation of the existing reader field, with no
change to its configured threshold. The frozen 1M/s run with sampled
peak 18435 still misses the limit by at least 18307 requests, and the
historical 5M/s run with sampled peak 4000404 by at least 4000276. The
sampling uncertainty is therefore immaterial to both optimization

*** Fractional-rate eligibility and the timestamp inverse

The plan timestamps ordinal i at floor(i * 10^9 / rate). The actual
driver and failure receipt both count 1 + floor(t * rate / 10^9),
capped at the planned total. This counts arrivals whose unrounded
timestamp is at most t. Inverting the stored floor timestamps instead
gives ceil((t + 1) * rate / 10^9), capped at the same total. At rate
3/s, the second planned timestamp is 333333333 ns and both existing
count expressions still report one due message at that instant. The
second becomes eligible at 333333334 ns under the existing rule.

For all supported rates the timestamp-derived count is ahead by at
most one; both counts coincide when the rate divides 10^9. Thus the
500k/s, 1M/s and 5M/s receipts retain the one-message sampling bound.
For a fractional rate, a successful sampled peak can understate the
peak against the stored timestamps by two messages: one across a
retirement and one from rounding. The actual loop and terminal count
expressions were extracted from the Scheme sources and checked under
installed Gerbil at 90 exact tick boundaries, with ordinals through
49999999. The existing native_source_plan model checks the inverse,
cap, integral rates and the two-message counterexample.

This is a qualification-contract discrepancy. The one-nanosecond
eligibility boundary cannot account for the observed benchmark backlog
at the three integral rates. Any future rounding correction needs to
cover both the driver and the failure receipt with the same convention
and preserve the integral path's generated cost.

** Frame occupancy bounds the pre-publication part of the tail

For a fully completed original run, let N be the successful samples,
E the measured inner elapsed time and H the physical frame count.
From enqueue through the recorded consumption timestamp each request
owns a frame. Those intervals lie inside E and at most H overlap.
Therefore at most floor(H*E/B) requests can spend B ns or more in
that interval. This remains true if readiness is out of order or a
request holds a frame even longer before actual retirement.

A p99 upper bucket edge P at 1 us resolution guarantees at least
N-ceil(0.99*N)+1 samples whose latency is at least P-1000 ns.
Subtract the maximum number of long frame holds from that tail count.
Each remaining sample must have waited at least P-1000-B ns before
publication. B is an analysis cutoff, not a timeout or a runtime
threshold. The proof requires the full successful corpus, original
planned-arrival latency and the matching frame limit. It cannot
reconstruct individual request timings.

* Complete-cycle ownership

The source owns planned arrival and the verified consumption transaction:
planned -> eligible -> reserved -> fully constructed -> published ->
worker execution/suspension -> ready -> received -> verified consumption ->
roots cleared and frame returned -> immediate eligible refill.

Publication is not completion. Dequeue and verified progress are not capacity
return. Cancellation of a caller does not permit reuse of a live worker's
frame. An unpublished construction failure retains its original identity and
planned timestamp; it must not advance the successful publication frontier.

For every consistent cut, due - completed = (due - published) +
(published - completed). The second term is bounded by physical frames. The
roughly four million 5M/s debt is consequently mostly unconstructed source
ordinal debt, not four million allocated payloads in Actor mailboxes.
Backpressure bounds retained objects; it does not erase planned-arrival delay.

Stateful work keeps Actor affinity. General Scheme handlers may suspend or
capture/re-enter continuations. No CPU-only optimization may infer otherwise
from one successful value test. Initialization, partial failure, cancellation,
old epochs, exception identity, unread results, multi-shot continuations,
explicit shutdown and join remain part of each proposed change.

* Capacity and pause conditions

Reuse actor_service_calculus.cycleCost: source + construction + two mailbox
transfers + execution + independent verification + consumption + scheduler
cost + observation. These phases share the same owner CPU. Taking their
maximum instead of their sum would invent pipeline parallelism.

| Offered/s | Available CPU budget/request at full availability | Ideal no-consumption pause spending all 128 messages |
|-----------+--------------------------------------------------+------------------------------------------------------|
| 500000 | 2 us | 256 us |
| 1000000 | 1 us | 128 us |
| 5000000 | 0.2 us | 25.6 us |

The CPU column is a necessary average budget, not a measured service cost.
The pause column is an optimistic continuous-arrival approximation starting
with zero debt; initial debt, bursts, packetization and service time consume
that allowance. The existing model uses explicit packetized bounds instead.

Recovery requires service rate mu strictly greater than arrival rate lambda;
a debt B needs approximately B/(mu-lambda) seconds under constant rates.
Finite owner availability and request fairness must also be qualified.
Weak fairness and a passing lifecycle invariant supply no microsecond bound.
No finite software queue can guarantee 128 planned outstanding messages under
unbounded owner unavailability.

The 5M/s observation completes near 1M/s and spends about five seconds draining
five million messages: sustained service deficit is directly visible. It
requires roughly five times the observed full-cycle capacity to keep pace,
not five times the Actor population. Exact phase CPU costs are still unknown.
The arrival-limited 1M/s CPU envelope also contains source pacing/yield/clock
work and setup/report work; it is not a saturated per-handler CPU measurement.
GC totals, remote CPU counters and maximum intervals cannot locate one request's
pause. Per-stage p99 values cannot be summed to explain end-to-end p99.

*** A common publication bound for the 5M/s cohort

For a fresh open-arrival op62 run, let T be the last planned tick,

hold over that prefix is at most F*T/(N-B). This optimistic bound
excludes time charged to the unfinished frames and is separate from
CPU or end-to-end latency.

* Work-sensitive service design

Selection must distinguish the following properties, using declared handler
and layout contracts at existing service/configuration boundaries.

| Work shape | Direction | Required limit/ownership | Explicit non-goal |
|------------+-----------+--------------------------+-------------------|
| Small CPU request | Preserve direct reusable-frame Mailbox cycle; eliminate demonstrated recurring business/bridge work | Full per-request CPU and allocation cost; actual consumption before reuse | No extra per-request mode check, queue or wake layer |
| Independently suspended requests | Scheme keeps distinct Actor continuations and runs eligible peers | Actor/stack/root memory and actual suspension; affinity and bounded live requests | Actor count does not create single-owner CPU parallelism |
| Repeated same-destination work | Consider bounded physical packet only where routing/order/suspension contracts allow | Count, bytes and execution quantum; individual result return; flush before dependency wait | No fill-to-batch barrier or delayed immediate refill |
| Stateful or unknown Scheme handler | Preserve pinned ownership and the general continuation-safe path | Actual owner and epoch through termination | No inferred non-suspension or post-entry reassignment |
| Large payload/long CPU turn | Bound retained bytes and declared work separately from request slots | Cooperative boundary and maximum indivisible work must be explicit | A C loop or envelope cannot make arbitrary Scheme work preemptible |

Actor population is separate from in-flight frames and runnable work.
The existing population model only permits cohort reconfiguration at a
quiescent boundary; this is not an implemented online autoscaler. Growth is
conditionally useful for blocked independent work within a memory budget.
No numeric automatic-growth formula or new default is admitted by this review.

For the frozen three-frame/four-destination round robin, each supplied group
of at most three goes to distinct targets. mailbox_packets already proves
that wrapping these requests without a valid routing change adds overhead.
The joint model also retains counterexamples where moving supply to workers
trades a request send for a credit wake, or waiting for ready siblings delays
refill. These are excluded routes, not new candidates.

A future packet may only be admitted if saved transfers exceed packaging,
decoding, singleton checks, routing setup, park/resume, FIFO handling,
register/stack/root lifetime and exception cleanup costs. Existing ready
results cannot be retained waiting for another member. The general single
result path must retain its cost and semantics.

** Same-Actor retirement for a closed private order turn

Operation 62 already owns the planned ordinal stream, creates its inputs
internally and chooses a private worker loop. Its generator creates a fresh
14-element vector with a zero wait field. The order kernel can suspend for
wait modes 1 and 2, and the general work-layout entry can install a
checkpoint. A same-Actor execution route is eligible only when the source
and retirement belong to the same Actor, the actual entry has no such
checkpoint, the input uses the closed no-wait profile, and the public
contract needs no per-request reply. A fixed layout or a successful native
type check alone provides none of those guarantees. Existing public,
stateful, wait-capable and unknown Scheme paths retain their own Actor
and continuation ownership.

The proposed route selects those premises at a private command boundary.
Within a bounded turn the service Actor reads due work, constructs a
fresh payload, invokes the same short-order handler, independently
verifies the result, records verified consumption, releases that item's
custody and immediately considers the next due request. A construction
failure does not advance the publication frontier. A verified result can
be timed before the progress operation, but a failing progress operation
still seals unreturned custody. Abort must remain distinct from verified
consumption, and an unstarted cancellation must not manufacture a
published item. Close must drain or terminate all live work before the
Actor is joined. This route removes per-order source-to-worker and
worker-to-source transport. The Actor still receives a command and
returns a terminal receipt through its Mailbox.

constructor. Their fixture-owned layout used the declared four-row span with no checkpoint.

checks inputs at the relevant ordinal limits. Each
processed three generations with the same verified digests, one
cancellation before publication, and one injected abort after the oracle.
The two modes both accounted for 69 publications, 68 verified returns
and one terminal abort. The separate worker arm used 138 request/reply

a cancellation must not revoke an already running handler, and any

and clock path still needs an implementation with real publication and
return cuts before any rate comparison. In particular, 5M/s allows
roughly 200 ns per offered item even with an uninterrupted owner.
Removing two sends alone gives no measured cost for construction,
business, independent validation, progress publication and timed
retirement. Qualification requires those complete costs and the same
clock, deadline, latency and debt reporting as the frozen workload.

** Payload reservation separate from frames and processor time

The Scheme order handler validates a two-element header plus three
signed-64 elements per row. Its result constructor produces 21
signed-64 elements for both the checked and native paths. The source
uses a four-row order in op62. The existing native access contract
also exercises a 146-element input, the 48-row layout, against both
handlers before the measured clock. The archived frozen 1M/s receipt
has data_access_edge_contract=true. It is a correctness witness for
the typed layout, rather than a bulk throughput measurement.

| One order | Input element bytes | Output element bytes | Sum reserved if both coexist | Three equal frame reservations |
|-----------+---------------------+----------------------+------------------------------+--------------------------------|
| 4 rows | 112 | 168 | 280 | 840 |
| 48 rows | 1168 | 168 | 1336 | 4008 |

The output can be allocated while input is still live. Overwriting a
frame's input slot with the result does not justify planning for only
the larger of those two vectors. The sums exclude vector headers,
envelopes, mailbox cells, accumulator objects, Actor stacks, runtime
heap and allocator slack. A bulk pool message can contain several
orders and needs a separate aggregate bound. These are not RSS limits.
At 5M short orders/s the elements would represent 1.4 GB/s materialized,
while the number of native frames stays fixed. That is a volume
calculation, not a claimed bandwidth or CPU ceiling.

The existing Actor custody model now has a weighted refinement with
independent frame and byte conditions. It charges the declared typed
payload before construction. The source must retain that reservation
through publication, suspension, readiness and caller cancellation,
then return it after verified consumption. A separately retained
continuation has its own reservation until it releases its roots.
The finite model gives one reserve per retained handle. An actual
profile needs a bound for every additional independently retained
graph, including repeated continuation returns, or it must preserve
the general path. No RSS or work estimate is inferred for an unknown
layout. No new runtime admission limit, Actor count or frame count is
configured by this change.

For a future variable-size path, separate the byte reservation from
the owner's execution budget. The row count is a declared unit of
work and does not predict CPU time for GC, suspension or arbitrary
Scheme code. Keep op62's fixed short-layout proof at setup where
possible. If an admission path later splits reservation from actual
construction, the byte charge must precede any allocation or handoff,
and an unpublished construction failure must undo only that reserve.

* Protocol-only batching ceiling

The existing packet-cost model now carries asymmetric request/reply costs
and nonnegative algebraic residuals. These are cost-envelope constraints,
not fitted native CPU predictions.

Let n be logical requests, k physical packets, b the maximum fully
materialized members per packet, R request-transfer cost, Q reply-transfer
cost, W unchanged per-request work and E all additional cost. All costs
are nonnegative and n <= b*k.

  original = n*(R+Q+W)
  request-packed = k*R+n*(Q+W)+E
  b*request-packed-original
    = (b*k-n)*R+(b-1)*n*(Q+W)+b*E >= 0

Thus request-only packing cannot exceed b-fold acceleration within this
cost envelope. With the frozen three-credit protocol and symmetric transfer
cost R=Q=u, the stronger residual is:

  3*request-packed-2*original = (3*k-n)*u+n*W+3*E >= 0

That symmetric upper bound is 1.5x. The asymmetric test explicitly exposes
why it cannot be used without qualifying transfer costs. Even hypothetically
packing both transfer legs with b=3 has an upper bound of 3x in the same
unchanged-cost model. Neither envelope supplies a fivefold improvement.

This is conditional on preserving the current relationship between native
credits and fully materialized packet members. It is not a universal limit
on Gerbil Actors, and it does not justify hardcoding an Actor population.
A lazy descriptor stream or changed credit protocol would need its own
construction/publication/failure and custody refinement; its saving cannot
be borrowed from this envelope. Enlarging a queue proves no CPU benefit.

The current round-robin supply has k=n within each three-frame group, so
there is no request-transfer saving even before adding packaging. This
eliminates unchanged-topology request packing as the next 5M/s architecture
candidate. Improving 1M/s tails and increasing sustained 5M/s capacity remain
different tasks with separate evidence requirements.

The complete mailbox_packets suite now passes 14 scenarios, including five
new cost-envelope checks. Bounded enumeration checks the residual identities
and counterexamples; the displayed algebra states their general premises.
No runtime speedup is inferred from those checks.

** Evidence-based ceiling on packing within a supply turn

The retained 7F op62 receipts count drive-loop observations above 128.
This bound applies only to complete scheduled runs with the original three
frames, ordinary replies, and immediate refill. At an above-limit turn,
due minus consumed is greater than 128 and no more than three published
requests are in custody. There is enough unpublished work to fill every
free frame. The original source then receives and consumes one reply,
leaving two charged frames. The next turn must receive another reply.
If it too starts above the limit, it can publish exactly one replacement.

Among N verified retirement turns, A above-limit turns can form at most
N-A+1 separate runs. At least max(0, 2*A-N-1) turns therefore have an
above-limit predecessor and supply exactly one message. Idle loops cannot
separate such consecutive retirements because the first turn leaves two
active frames. Even if every other message could be grouped with a valid
same-turn destination, a maximum packet of three saves at most
floor(2*(N-max(0,2*A-N-1))/3) request sends.

** Exact rank recording with bounded sequential storage

The frozen source allocates 80000000 bytes of latency buckets before its
service clock starts. Each retirement reads a selected bucket and writes
its increment before announcing verified progress. The terminal report
can contain that timed sample even when progress publication fails and
the source has not returned the frame. Neither buffer size nor sample
population tracks unpublished source debt.

At the accepted command bounds of 5000000 offered per second for at
most ten seconds, one bucket can hold at most 50000000 samples.
Replacing the u64 bins with u32 therefore preserves exact counts and
halves bin bytes. The installed Gerbil oracle comparison covered an
empty histogram, split ranks, the final bucket, and 50000000 samples in
one bucket. Both widths returned the same values. Gambit generated the
same two vector calls, addition and two poll sites in the small checked
increment fixture, with only the typed access targets changed. This
option saves memory and retains the read-modify-write. It also requires
compatibility work for the existing u64 terminal consumer.

A second design uses a private u32 sample vector sized once at
initialization. Store the existing microsecond bucket at index
=completed= before calling the current verified-progress operation.
This replaces a read, addition and store with an indexed store, without
moving the sampling point or changing credit return. At 1M/s and 5M/s
for one second the vector's element storage is 4000000 and 20000000
bytes respectively. At 20000000 planned arrivals its vector alone
equals the dense histogram, so the dense mode remains necessary for
larger profiles. Cold reducer scratch also counts when choosing a mode.
The committed count and terminal timed length remain separate. An
ordinary finish reads exactly =completed= elements. An explicit
progress-deadline branch after the write seals =completed+1= timed
samples with the original committed count. A failure before the write
seals only =completed=. An exception must retain its original
identity; transfer requires the existing exclusive aborting-source
proof and a command-bound single take. The generic histogram contract
continues to retain its observable typed snapshot.

A separate GCC/O2 C fixture recovers exact p99 and p99.9 using two
passes over 24-bit bucket indices, with 49152 bytes of reducer scratch.

suffixes for just the selected prefixes. It agreed with an independent
sort in 4100 cases, including ranks in one prefix or different prefixes,

=.ci/mailbox-send-target-20261107/histogram-width.yGMoLp=.
The small linker fixture needed the previously verified Apple/Homebrew

** Resident worker ownership across source invocations

The current op62 creates four workers and binds its source
mailbox before the source clock opens. Warmup and main each
create their own cohort. A resident-worker design would
remove repeated cohort startup and preserve a known mailbox
waiter across calls. This could change early availability;
it supplies no evidence of lower steady-state cycle CPU.
The earlier general service-binding variant remains rejected.

The existing Actor population model now separates worker
birth epoch from the source invocation epoch. After every
published job is consumed and all waiting, ready and retained
state has drained, a new invocation can reset its ordinal
ledger while keeping the same physical worker. The work model
resets only per-invocation construction, verification and
transfer counts. Actor creation stays cumulative. The native
model scenario reuses the same ordinal on the same worker,
rejects the previous invocation's resume, accepts the current
one and joins the one created worker at close.

Installed Gerbil provides two independent functional witnesses.
The first reuses one worker for three invocations of ordinal
zero and checks original exception identity, GC, stale
generation rejection and clean join. The second uses the
frozen op62 reply representation and slot/ID/frame/state
check. A reply kept from the old invocation has the same
ordinal and slot, even after its old frame is deliberately
restored to ready. Fresh invocation frames make its object
identity fail, so the existing per-reply check can fence old
replies without adding a per-message epoch field. This is
transport and predicate evidence, not a native embedded
