* Architecture and cost contract

Scheme selects an execution plan once at handler entry. The application declares
its total logical work. If this fits the configured maximum and no opposite-class
waiter, unstarted short dispatch or unoffered short-ready generation exists,
Scheme selects the complete handler span. Otherwise it selects a contended span.
The lexical =with-gerbil-rs-service-work-plan= macro expands the charge operation
in Scheme; there is no per-record procedure allocation, Rust callback, modulo
check or fixed four-record poll in a whole-handler arithmetic loop. Entry
selection, one terminal charge and existing lease-release accounting remain.

The directed workload uses contended work 4 and maximum uninterrupted work 48.
A bulk order contains 48 records; a short order contains 4. A batch of eight
orders is not one 384-record indivisible span. Each order retains the existing
handler lease release and Actor scheduling boundary. Rust admission still retains
8 bulk items or 1 short item through independent result retirement. Executed
records, admission units and item completion IDs have separate authorities.

A short request arriving after a whole span has already been selected can wait
for that one span of at most 48 records. The protocol does not promise to preempt
it after four records. At a contended boundary, existing Scheme suspension and
class arbitration provide the short worker an execution opportunity. A terminal
span proceeds to result publication without an unnecessary precompletion yield.
Result harvesting, projection, root release and capacity retirement retain their
existing ownership boundaries.

This is a cooperative logical-work bound, not a wall-time deadline. Arbitrary
Scheme handlers, allocation, result construction, GC, OS pauses and I/O are not
preempted. Continuation reentry preserves the selected bounded span; replacing a
policy makes old charges inert through its identity fence. Scheme retains its
complete language/runtime capability. Rust only distributes across instances.

** Architectural consequence

Do not run the combined publication comparison or repeat this unchanged
candidate hoping for a favorable window. CPU throughput is not preserved and
mixed short-tail behavior is not stable enough to admit the patch stack. Defaults
remain unchanged. Historical strict-span and joint-dispatch negatives remain
independent evidence; comparing their percentages across captures is not a
causal speedup measurement.

The record loop now avoids repeated fixed checks on more than 99% of requests.
That observation rules out treating a larger span or a different yield threshold
as a demonstrated solution. Entry plan selection, context/owner validation and
plan/accounting writes still occur for every handler. The next architectural
candidate should let Scheme lease/scheduling transitions own the plan state,
invalidate it on dispatch, queue and ready-generation changes, and reuse the
uncontended compiled handler until those events change its obligations. Cold
validation must remain at the public configuration boundary. Work already
selected retains its declared maximum; a cached plan must never erase a late
short obligation, continuation identity fence or independent result retirement.
This direction is not yet implemented or accepted.

The current capture cannot establish the exclusive cost of entry selection,
bookkeeping, OS scheduling or GC. Admission and native construction wall tails
also increase in this capture; their scopes overlap other stages and are not
additive. Do not subtract pooled quantiles or claim that moving plan selection
alone will solve those tails. The unresolved watchdog failures retain their
original identities and unverified cleanup state; this successful A/B does not
attribute them.
