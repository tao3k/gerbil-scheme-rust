* Why earlier traces were insufficient

The original broad System Trace ended acquisition but failed finalization
within its controller limit. Reduced Time Profiler plus Thread State Trace
could save readable scheduling tables. This was not sufficient by itself:
short rolling windows sometimes discarded owner creation/identity metadata,
leaving sentinel thread rows despite valid clock signposts. Starting capture
after owner initialization also produced missing identity. Refreshing the
unchanged thread name did not repair that loss and was removed.

Unwindowed or larger full-repetition captures still exceeded their 45-second
diagnostic controller; one overlapping-recorder attempt is not accepted as
independent evidence. Recorder sampling showed a DTX connection semaphore
wait at the sampled instant; later runs progressed into serialization. These
are controller/finalization failures, not proof of permanent deadlock. Traces,
compiler and harness failures are retained privately.

The accepted route uses the reduced instrument set, launches the benchmark
under xctrace, and captures a diagnostic single A/B per load. Payload sizes,
warmups, native settings, VM identities and progress limits are preserved.
Two captures saved/exported successfully with target exit zero and clean
four-owner shutdown: the first used a two-second rolling window, the second
five seconds. The latter retained owner creation through recorder flush and
completed serialization after an observed 15-second artifact-progress receipt.
A five-second window is a qualified setting for this directed capture, not a
guarantee that arbitrary longer workloads retain their metadata.

The observation flag is explicit and defaults off. The ordinary performance
benchmark keeps all repetitions. =--trace-once= without observation is rejected.
No Bash driver, new package dependency or alternative Scheme compiler is added.

* Clock and coverage admission

Each observation reset emits an Apple =os_signpost_event_emit= marker on its
fixed owner. A native row =[before_ns, after_ns, tid]= bounds the emission.
The log object is released after each marker. The macro is compiled by the
existing host C compiler helper because Gambit's configured GCC does not
support Apple's log-format builtin. Scheme still compiles through Gerbil,
Gambit C and its configured native compiler; native optimization remains O2.

The bench emits native anchor rows through the existing typed control path.
Wait-event width stays six and kinds remain unchanged. Bazel now declares
qualification C/header/Scheme inputs in its existing source group; the
handwritten bridge has an explicit source exception to generated-C ignores.

Markers are matched by both TID and native clock, not clock alone. The native
monotonic resolution measured 1000 ns. Intersected offset bounds, including
that resolution, gave approximately +/-1.000 us and +/-1.104 us for the two
captures. The per-request Instant/native anchor added at most 666 ns / 416 ns,
plus one native clock tick; the conservative combined alignment boundary was
below 3 us, not just the kernel clock-offset boundary. Both retained all four
owner TIDs and 16 timed-sample anchors.

Each capture covered all 256 short-request ingress intervals and complete
request intervals (four full-size samples, 64 probes each). Analysis resolved
XML references, checked nonoverlapping owner state spans and rejected missing
owner identities. The second capture covered 360 distinct overlapping native
phase spans. These are interval measurements, unlike earlier Mach state points.

* What the intervals actually show

The second capture's per-class/arm ingress attribution is below. Totals span
64 probes in each row. Times are microseconds and approximate at the stated
clock boundary; Preempted/Interrupted remain separate from Runnable.

| Load/arm | Maximum ingress | Total Running | Total Blocked | Total Runnable |
|----------+-----------------+---------------+---------------+----------------|
| CPU fixed supply | 418.5 | 1583.0 | 39.5 | 70.3 |
| CPU pooled supply | 388.5 | 3409.7 | 34.9 | 105.2 |
| Mixed fixed supply | 532.9 | 1794.4 | 25.2 | 54.9 |
| Mixed pooled supply | 173.7 | 1049.8 | 32.0 | 76.9 |

The largest individual ingress Runnable contribution was 11.3 us and Blocked
8.2 us in this capture. The longest mixed ingress interval was 532.9 us:
503.3 us Running, 21.6 us Interrupted and 8.0 us Preempted. It is not a
half-millisecond scheduler-ready-queue delay.

Complete request boundaries are reconstructed from the existing start-relative
entry marks and actual/construct/pending/projection durations, not by assuming
caller wake-return precedes owner execution. All 256 complete request intervals
were covered. Representative tails from the second capture:

| Case | Actual wall us | Construction wall / CPU us | Pending us | GC wall delta us | Kernel evidence |
|------+----------------+----------------------------+------------+------------------+-----------------|
| CPU pooled, owner 0 | 523.5 | 421.3 / 420.6 | 30.9 | 412.0 during construction | 493.5 Running; 13.7 Blocked; 14.5 Preempted |
| Mixed pooled, owner 1 | 509.8 | 3.1 / 2.5 | 485.0 | 443.0 through pending | 501.2 Running; 4.6 Blocked; 3.1 Runnable |
| Mixed fixed, owner 3 | 492.0 | 2.5 / 1.8 | 477.5 | 454.2 through pending | 490.6 Running; 1.3 Interrupted |

GC-associated work can occur during construction, Scheme advance, projection
or while another request is pending. The first capture also found a 453.8 us
ingress interval almost entirely Running, overlapping a Submit phase with
439.9 us GC wall and 454.0 us owner CPU. Thus these captured tails are chiefly
on-core work, not missing wakeups. This is a bounded inference from matching
intervals and enclosing counters, not an exact collector-only CPU measurement.

Genuine blocking is separately visible: one native select span lasted 5471.0 us,
with 5459.8 us Blocked, 6.3 us Runnable and 4.9 us Running; its native wrapper
measured 6.5 us owner CPU. Another lasted 4489.0 us, mostly Blocked. These waits
were not the half-millisecond short-request CPU tails above. Native service IO
and cooperative sleep can legitimately block; a blocked interval alone does
not establish starvation or a supply-protocol defect.

SDK GC CPU remains process-level accounting. It is neither summed over owners
nor subtracted from wall time. Per-request/phase thread CPU is enclosing owner
CPU, not just that request's Scheme handler or collector. Kernel Runnable is
not Running, and neither is inferred by wall-minus-CPU subtraction.

The original tens-of-milliseconds tail did not recur in these two diagnostic
captures. Observation changes costs and scheduling. These traces cannot rule
out OS delays in other executions or stand in for unobserved performance.

* Actual routing prototype and directed A/B

The rejected prototype published an opt-in advisory hint around owner Submit,
Advance and Retire turns, including type construction and projection. A scope
guard restored it on normal return and unwind. Short routing kept quota and
short-request priority first, then used the hint before native occupancy;
batch ranking, rotating ties, native reservation and FIFO admission remained
unchanged. A Scheme wait may itself execute Scheme, so a clear hint never
promised an idle VM. It granted no credit and migrated no native state.

Both arms used pooled bounded supply, actual NativePort readback, cooperative
budget one, feed one, coalesced notification, four live VMs and two Tokio workers.
Each capture reused those same VM identities across both arms. Loads were
8192 orders of 48 lines, batches of eight and 64 four-line short probes with
nominal 500 us spacing. Slots four/work 32, maximum three active bulk jobs per
owner, and staging 128 callbacks/1024 work units were identical. Original
watchdogs were unchanged. Actual constructed router modes were recorded after
timing. No own profiler/compiler/model ran during the timed captures.

A is ReselectProgress; B is the rejected service-phase hint candidate. Each
capture contains 40 samples, alternating arm order, and clean four-owner exit.

| Capture/load | Orders/s A -> B | Actual short p99 ms A -> B | Min normalized progress % A -> B | Finish spread ms A -> B |
|--------------+-----------------+----------------------------+----------------------------------+-------------------------|
| A CPU | 272528 -> 287376 | 4.118 -> 1.044 | 62.70 -> 98.54 | 13.658 -> 1.730 |
| A mixed | 55170 -> 55327 | 1.651 -> 1.732 | 98.80 -> 98.80 | 1.479 -> 1.534 |
| B CPU | 302190 -> 294246 | 1.193 -> 1.149 | 98.67 -> 98.56 | 0.744 -> 1.125 |
| B mixed | 56925 -> 54874 | 1.760 -> 1.599 | 98.76 -> 98.79 | 1.570 -> 5.422 |

CPU throughput changed +5.45% then -2.63%; mixed changed +0.29% then -3.60%.
Mixed p99 changed +4.91% then -9.14%. The large first CPU p99 improvement
coincided with a poor baseline fairness sample and did not repeat in magnitude.
Second mixed finish spread grew more than threefold. No stable joint
throughput/tail/fairness improvement was established; no hint API is retained.

A phase snapshot is not a prediction of the next allocation or GC pause, and
four VMs still share physical CPU/cache resources. Replacing the route's key
alone does not give short jobs a bounded runtime service duration. The remaining
optimization gate is low-intrusion attribution of the unobserved larger tails,
then an explicit allocation/service-cost contract and matched native experiment;
do not infer a GC budget or hard time bound from declared item count alone.
